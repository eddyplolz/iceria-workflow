The '''World Standards''' are the shared technical rules of the world of Iceria: how seas,
borders, dates, names and records work, so that every nation uses the same conventions. They are
referenced by clause 1.7 of the [[Charter]].

''Until Iceria opens, these standards are a working draft and may change at any time.''

Your [[Nation proposals|proposal]] has six parts, and the rows above show where each comes from:
the nation's name (Names), its claim and its capital (Land), its culture (People), a short history
with dates (Past), and an answer to at least one of three questions: how it differs from its Earth
anchor (10.1), one problem it faces in 2000, or one neighbor or region it deals with.

== 1. How these standards work ==

'''1.1''' Every nation follows these standards. They are the world's defaults, not a nation's laws.

'''1.2''' Two or more nations may agree to something different '''between themselves''' by treaty, as
long as it doesn't affect anyone else.

'''1.3''' The stewards, who admit nations and keep the map ([[Charter|charter]] 2.1), keep these standards and change them the ordinary way ([[Charter|charter]] 9.2): a public
proposal, at least 7 days of comment, then a decision.

== 2. Seas (modeled on UNCLOS) ==

'''2.1''' '''Baseline.''' A nation's sea zones are measured from its coastline on the official map. A bay
whose mouth is 24 nautical miles wide or less may be closed with a straight line.

'''2.2''' '''Zones,''' measured from the baseline:

{| class="wikitable"
! Zone !! Width !! What the coastal nation controls
|-
| Internal waters || inside the baseline || everything, like land
|-
| Territorial sea || 12 nautical miles || full sovereignty, subject to innocent passage (2.4)
|-
| Contiguous zone || 24 nautical miles || customs, immigration, and health enforcement
|-
| Exclusive economic zone || 200 nautical miles || fishing, seabed resources, and energy
|}
'''2.3''' '''Overlaps.''' Where two nations' zones overlap, the border is the line halfway between their
coasts, unless they agree otherwise by treaty. Land not yet written (section 12) counts as a
neighbor: its coast takes its side of the line, and its waters belong to the states that live there.
A nation's waters therefore do not change when a neighboring land is later claimed.

'''2.4''' '''Passage.''' Ships of every nation may pass peacefully through any territorial sea. Straits
used for international navigation stay open to transit.

'''2.5''' '''Islands.''' An island that can sustain habitation gets full zones. A rock that can't gets a
territorial sea only.

'''2.6''' '''The high seas.''' Everything beyond the exclusive economic zones belongs to no one and is open
to all.

'''2.7''' '''The official sea map.''' The stewards may publish the zones on a maritime map drawn from the
official claims. That map settles disputes about where a zone lies.

== 3. The polar regions ==

'''3.1''' '''The polar ice is a Polar Treaty Zone,''' modeled on the Antarctic Treaty: the ice sheets of
Hyperbora and The Austrine, as drawn on the claims map (7.4).
No nation may claim sovereignty there, and its coasts generate no sea zones for anyone.

'''3.2''' The Polar Treaty Zone is for '''peaceful and scientific use''': research stations, expeditions,
and cooperation. Military bases are not allowed.

'''3.3''' Stations and expeditions in the Polar Treaty Zone are registered on the wiki's [[Polar Register]].
The [[Concord of Nations]], the world's international body, founded in 1945
(13.1; [[Charter|charter]] 8.2), looks after the Polar Treaty Zone.

'''3.4''' '''The polar tundra''' around the ice is ordinary land: nations may claim it under the normal
rules.

== 4. Land borders and airspace ==

'''4.1''' '''Land borders''' are the claim lines on the official map.

'''4.2''' Where a border follows a '''river''', it runs down the middle of the main channel; along a
'''mountain range''', it follows the crest line. Across a lake, it follows the median line, every point of which is equally far from the nearest shore of each side. A treaty may set a different line.

'''4.3''' '''Airspace''' covers a nation's land, internal waters, and territorial sea. Above the high seas
and the Polar Treaty Zone, airspace is international.

== 5. Time, dates, units, and coordinates ==
[[File:Iceria-reference-lines.png|thumb|upright=1.4|Zone 0 is centered on the prime meridian, which crosses the equator in Mesaria]]

'''5.1''' '''Calendar.''' As in [[Charter|charter]] 1.6: two real months are one in-world year of twelve months, starting
from the year 2000. Dates are written "14 March 1998". The official calendar is the ordinary twelve
months used on Earth, and Iceria keeps Earth's clock: the same day, the same year and one moon, Mene. A nation may
keep a local calendar for flavor, with its own month names, era or new year, but it counts the same days,
because time moves at one rate for everyone. Official dates use the common calendar. Years before its epoch ([[History of Iceria]]) are written
BC, as in "480 BC"; later years carry no era. Periods in these
standards are real time unless they say in-world.

'''5.2''' '''Time zones''' (guidance). Most nations use one of 24 standard time zones, each 15° of longitude
wide, with zone 0 centered on the map's prime meridian (0°). A nation chooses its own legal time.

'''5.3''' '''Units.''' Metric (SI) units, plus nautical miles for distances at sea (1 nautical mile = 1.852
km).

'''5.4''' '''Coordinates''' use latitude and longitude on the canonical map. Areas in square kilometers
reported by the Claims Desk, the stewards' tool that checks and records claims ([[Charter|charter]]
3.2), are official.

== 6. Names and languages ==

'''6.1''' A nation's place names should fit its own language, whether invented or inspired by a real
one.

'''6.2''' A nation does not take a real country's name, so the map never reads as Earth relabeled.
Other real names, city names among them, may be used where they fit the nation's language (6.1).

'''6.3''' The canonical geography names are fixed: the continents, polar regions, oceans and seas, and
the largest lakes, islands, rivers and mountain ranges named on the official map.

'''6.4''' A new, unmapped feature is named by the first nation to publish the name on the wiki, on its own land, on unclaimed land (provisionally, below), or elsewhere with the agreement of the nation holding that land. A name on unclaimed land is provisional: the nation that later claims the land may rename it.

'''6.5''' Two nations may not use the same name for a nation or a capital. Other shared names are
told apart on the wiki with the nation in brackets: a town called Valmont in two nations would be
"Valmont (Alexandria)" and "Valmont (Mantua)".

== 7. The wiki and the map ==

'''7.1''' Each kind of page uses its standard infobox: '''Infobox Country''' for a nation, '''Infobox
Person''' for a person, and '''Infobox Settlement''' for a place.

'''7.2''' '''[[The Compass]]''' is Iceria's news record. Each in-world month has a page ("The Compass/March
2000") listing its stories by topic, and each year's page ("The Compass/2000") gathers its months. A longer
story is its own page under its year ("The Compass/2000/Title") and uses the '''Compass story template''',
which lists it on its month's page.

'''7.3''' '''Flags''' (guidance). Most flags are rectangles in a 2:3 or 1:2 ratio, uploaded as SVG or as a PNG
at least 600 pixels wide.

'''7.4''' '''Official maps.''' Iceria has two official maps, each for its own job. The world map is
official for the land, the sea and the names of places; the claims map made by the Claims Desk is
official for borders. Nations may draw their own detailed maps, but the official maps win wherever
they disagree.

== 8. Economy yardsticks ==

'''8.1''' Each nation chooses its own currency, or shares one with other nations by agreement. To compare
economies, figures are also given in '''International Reference Units (IRU)'''. One IRU buys about what one
US dollar bought in the year 2000.

'''8.2''' '''The plausibility guideline.''' Most nations fall within these ranges; a nation outside them
explains why:

{| class="wikitable"
! Measure !! Plausible range
|-
| Population density, national average || 1 to 1,500 people per km²
|-
| GDP per person || 500 to 120,000 IRU
|-
| Military spending || 0.5% to 10% of GDP
|}
For example, a nation of 20 million people on 400,000 km² (50 people per km²), with 12,000 IRU
per person and 2% of GDP on defense, sits well inside every range.

'''8.3''' '''Tiers.''' The stewards set each nation's tier ([[Charter|charter]] 3.7) from its total GDP in IRU, as the GDP in its '''Infobox Country''' (7.1) gives it:
* a '''great power''' at 3 trillion IRU or more;
* a '''regional power''' from 300 billion IRU up to 3 trillion;
* a '''local power''' under 300 billion IRU.

A tier describes a nation's weight in the world and sets its strength on the [[Dice table]] ([[Charter|charter]] 3.9); it grants or limits nothing else. A steward logs each tier on the [[Iceria log]] with the GDP figure it rests on, and a tier changes only when a steward logs a new figure; a nation may ask for that at any time. A steward logs a figure only once the nation's population density and GDP per person fall within the ranges in 8.2, or the nation has explained why they do not. A war uses the tiers logged when both sides accepted the dice, and a nation with no tier logged is given one before it accepts them. A tier may be appealed within 14 days of being logged, as in [[Charter|charter]] 6.6, and a steward never sets the tier of a nation they run ([[Charter|charter]] 2.4).

== 9. Diplomatic basics ==

'''9.1''' '''Treaty format''' (guidance): a title, the parties, the in-world date, numbered articles, and the
signatories.

'''9.2''' '''A treaty is canon''' once every party has agreed to it and it is published on the wiki's
[[Treaty Register]]. A party leaves a treaty by adding a dated withdrawal to the treaty's page; what that
means in-world is for the nations' stories.

'''9.3''' '''Recognition''' is each nation's own choice. Refusing to recognize a nation doesn't remove it
from the map or from canon.

'''9.4''' '''Embassies and consulates''' (guidance) open by agreement between the two nations.

== 10. Culture zones ==
[[File:Iceria-culture-zones.png|thumb|upright=1.6|The culture-zone map: each zone is the home of one culture family]]

'''10.1''' '''The [[:File:Iceria-culture-zones.png|culture-zone map]]''' shows where Iceria's peoples live, so a new nation can see at a
glance where its culture belongs. It divides the claimable land into 31 zones. Each zone is the
home of a culture family with an in-world name, modeled on one or more Earth cultures, its
anchors.

'''10.2''' '''A strong default.''' A nation takes its zone's culture, or explains its twist in one line
(for example, "Thulean settlers on a tropical coast"). The stewards may approve other exceptions.

'''10.3''' '''Fusion at the edges.''' Where zones meet, mixed cultures are expected.

'''10.4''' '''The frontier continents.''' On Septentria, Meridia and Selenara, each zone names its native
peoples and the settlers who came from the Old World. By 2000 some native nations are independent
and others live inside settler states. A nation there may be native, settler or mixed.

'''10.5''' '''History.''' Iceria's age of sail followed Earth's: first contact around 1450 to 1550,
colonies through the 1700s, and independence between about 1780 and 1970. Its conquests,
epidemics and forced labor, including a slave trade between the continents, are part of the
record. No nation has to roleplay them, and players may explore them with care.

'''10.6''' '''The official culture-zone map''' is drawn by the stewards from the list below. It settles
which zone a place lies in.

== 11. Growing a nation ==

'''11.1''' '''Grow by developing.''' A nation may claim more land ([[Charter|charter]] 3.5) only once the land it
already holds is written up: region pages that together cover at least half of it. Written-up land also
stays with a nation that goes dormant ([[Charter|charter]] 4.3), until it has been dormant for a year and
is released ([[Charter|charter]] 4.4).

'''11.2''' '''A region page''' is a wiki page about one part of the nation, such as a province, state or
county. It counts when it runs to about 300 words or more and says where the region is, what its
land is like, who lives there, and its main town. No single region page counts for more than a quarter
of the nation's land.

'''11.3''' '''Region outlines.''' The member paints each region's outline in the [https://studio.iceria.org Claim Studio], as for a
claim; the Claims Desk measures it and records it, so nobody measures by hand. The region page gives the
area the Desk measured and shows the region on a map.

'''11.4''' '''Asking to expand.''' The expansion request lists the region pages; the Claims Desk adds up how
much of the land they cover, and any page that falls short is named, so the nation knows what to finish.

'''11.5''' '''Everyone.''' This applies to every nation, the founder's included.

== 12. Land not yet written ==

'''12.1''' '''Lived on, not yet written.''' In 2000 all claimable land is inhabited and governed. The
culture zones (section 10) and, on the frontier continents, the frontier table in [[Culture zones of Iceria]] say who lives
where; the names, borders, governments and histories of unclaimed lands are not canon until
someone writes them. A claim writes a country into the record. On the official map, unclaimed
land carries a faint tint of its culture zone.

'''12.2''' '''Generic mentions.''' A story may mention unwritten lands in general terms ("a neighboring
state", "trade with the Ausonic coast") without asking anyone.

'''12.3''' '''Contact stubs.''' When a story needs a named unwritten country (a trade partner, a rival, a
border incident), the player writes a one-paragraph stub: its name, culture zone, rough location, and the event the story needs. It may be used straight away and stands unless a steward objects within 3 days.
A stub stays claimable: whoever later claims that land may rewrite anything in it except the events that other nations' published stories already rely on, such as a trade deal or a border incident. Neither a stub nor a story about it can fix a lasting relationship for that country (colony, protectorate, vassal, ally or enemy) or its history beyond those events (see 13.6). A war or an annexation against an unwritten country
needs a steward's approval; growing into unclaimed land under [[Charter|charter]] 3.5 is not an annexation.

'''12.4''' '''The [[Concord of Nations]]''' has many unwritten member states. Only player nations' votes and
speeches are played; unwritten members always abstain. Each member has one vote. A resolution binds only the members that vote for it. Any nation may ask to speak on a matter that touches it; refusing the request takes a majority of the other members voting for or against, and a tie lets the nation speak. Unlike a resolution, a refusal binds every member.

'''12.5''' '''Exploration''' means surveying, mapping and naming detail in lands that
are lived on, not finding empty land.

== 13. History ==

'''13.1''' '''The shared history.''' The broad strokes every nation shares are in [[History of Iceria]]: humankind's origin in Mesaria, the four river cradles, the trade seas,
the age of sail on two oceans, independence, the Great War, ending in 1945, and the Concord of
Nations founded that year. A nation's own history must fit them.

'''13.2''' '''What stays open.''' The page names no empire, kingdom or state except Leria, the
classical republic and empire that [[Alexandria]] wrote and descends from, Alexandria itself
(13.6), and those the founding nations wrote ([[Charter|charter]] 3.11). The names of all other empires, kingdoms and states, and which of them a nation descends
from, belong to the nations that write them (6.4 and 12).

'''13.3''' '''Faiths.''' The great faiths have in-world names anchored on Earth faiths (Soterian,
Diathecan, Deditan, Ritan, Vigilan, the Lararian rites). Each nation writes its own doctrines and
practice.

'''13.4''' '''The calendar''' is in 5.1: the year 2000 is the first year of play ([[Charter|charter]] 1.6).

'''13.5''' '''Technology''' in each in-world year matches Earth's of the same year. A nation may lead in a field it has
written up, by a few in-world years at most; anything beyond Earth is local canon (section 14). No nation is proven to hold a
nuclear weapon of its own: the warheads built in the Great War are held in Concord custody under the [[International Nuclear Compact]],
and a few unwritten countries are suspected of hidden programs. No player nation starts with a nuclear weapon, and the
stewards may open nuclear weapons to established nations by a change to these standards.

[[File:Iceria-holy-lands-lerian-empire.png|thumb|upright=1.6|The Lerian Empire at its greatest extent (13.6)]]
'''13.6''' '''Hard history.''' A nation may have been a colonizer, a colony, a protectorate, a trading
partner or never colonized. No nation makes another player's nation its colonizer, its enslaver or
its wartime enemy without that player's agreement.
* Where a written nation's history reaches land that no player has written yet, the player who later writes a nation there chooses its part in that history; a contact stub (12.3) fixes only the events a published story needs.
* [[Alexandria]]'s history is the first such case, with one fixed point: Leria's rule. Leria was the classical republic and empire Alexandria descends from, and the [[Geography of Iceria|Thalassine]] is the sea at the middle of the world and the Ecliptic the busiest sea of the Old World:
** all land inside the Lerian Empire's greatest extent, shown on the map of the Lerian Empire, was ruled by Leria. A nation whose land lies there writes Leria into the history of that land, unless its page already does, and chooses its part in that rule: a loyal province, a client kingdom, a frontier that rose against it or a land that broke away early, and when that rule ended, as the empire gave up its provinces from about 450 on;
** a nation on Mesaria's Numidic coast outside that extent chooses whether its ancestors were Leria's allies, its rivals or apart from it;
** a nation inside the Lerian Empire's greatest extent or in Alexandria's former colonies chooses whether its church follows the Pontiff of Leria;
** a nation across the Thalassine, on Mesaria's coasts or in the Ecliptic islands chooses whether it was an Alexandrian colony.

== 14. Beyond Earth ==

'''14.1''' '''The shared world is Earth-like.''' Everything the nations share follows Earth's rules: the map, the
climate, the shared history (section 13), technology (13.5) and wildlife. Wildlife is Earth's, plus local species
that could plausibly have evolved where they live, such as a large flightless island bird; never monsters.

'''14.2''' '''Local canon.''' Inside its own borders, a nation may write things beyond Earth: an undying ruler, an
unusual technology, a people who are not human, a faith whose miracles work. Such '''local canon''' must be
consistent and come with costs, and it stays local: nothing may change the shared world if it were true, whether
its map, climate or geology, contact with other worlds, travel faster than light, or a power or cure that spreads
abroad. Local canon works best when it is rare: most of a nation is ordinary, with one or two
elements beyond Earth. For example, a queen may have reigned unaging since 1820, in a kingdom that is
otherwise ordinary, while her court must keep the secret from foreign envoys. A joke nation is not local canon. The stewards approve local canon when it is declared: it must be well
developed, evidenced on the nation's own pages, and good writing.

'''14.3''' '''Other nations opt in.''' Local canon counts toward another nation only when that nation agrees, for the
stories it agrees to (1.2). Toward a nation that has not agreed, local canon reads as its nearest Earth equivalent:
a strange warship is a warship, an undying queen is a long-reigning one, and the nation's own news is its own
account of itself.

'''14.4''' '''No extra strength.''' Local canon is story, not power. The plausibility guideline (8.2), tiers and the
[[Dice table]] apply unchanged. Two nations that have both opted in may agree their own rules between themselves.

'''14.5''' '''Where it is recorded.''' A nation names its local canon in its proposal, and the [[Local canon register]]
lists it with the nations that have opted in. The nation's own pages are written in-world, like any article.

'''14.6''' '''The moon.''' [[Mene]] is part of the shared world (14.1). It has the size, mass, orbit and airless
surface of Earth's Moon, and is called the moon in everyday speech. Mene belongs to no state and cannot be claimed.
Crews landed on it before 2000; the nations that sent them, and where they landed, are entered on the Mene Register, and each of those nations names its own landing site. Its seas, basins, highlands and ranges keep the names on the Mene maps: Ecliptic-coast
sailors and astronomers named those of the near side, and the astronomers who first mapped the far side named its
features. Craters and smaller features take the name the first nation to publish one gives them, entered on the Mene
Register; Mene names are final, since no nation can claim the ground. Until Iceria opens, though, the large near-side craters the Mene Register lists as reserved are held from open naming for the nations the Register assigns them to, each of which names its own; any left unnamed then are open like every other crater. Seen from Iceria, Mene and Alteia both appear
about half a degree across.

'''14.7''' '''The sun.''' [[Alteia]] is part of the shared world (14.1). It is in every physical respect
Earth's Sun: the same size, mass, makeup, rotation, age, light and activity. Iceria circles it in Earth's
orbit, with Earth's tilt. Everyday speech calls it the sun, with the adjective solar; Alteian is kept for a
few astronomers' terms, such as the Alteian cycle of sunspots, about 11 years long. Other planets circle
Alteia; none is named, and none may be named or settled. Eclipses of the sun and of the moon (14.6) happen
as on Earth.
