This manual is the house style for articles on Iceria. It covers an article from its title to its last category, and each rule comes with its reason and an example, so that a writer who follows it produces an article that reads like every other good article here. The box below is the short version; the sections after it explain each rule.

The manual governs articles. Proposals, talk pages and the pages about the project itself, such as the [[Charter]] and the [[World Standards]], may speak about Iceria as a project, and their own conventions apply there. Stories, poems and other works in the Library follow it only in the frame around their text (18).

== 1. Voice ==
=== 1.1 The encyclopedia inside the world ===
Iceria's articles are written as an encyclopedia published inside Iceria. To the writer the world is real: its nations exist, its history happened and its people live there. An article never steps outside that world. It does not mention members, proposals, claims or the drawing of the map, it gives no dates from the real calendar, it compares nothing with a place on Earth ("the size of Belgium", "a second Malta"), and it does not treat a nation's arrival on the wiki as an event in the nation's history.

That kind of talk has its own places: a nation's proposal, its meta page, talk pages, the [[Water cooler]] and the project pages. The text of a reference may also name a proposal or the Charter (13.1), because a reference is where a reader goes to check a fact, not part of the story. A work in the Library carries a credit to the member who wrote it at its foot (18.2).

Each nation may have a meta page in the project space, titled ''The World of Iceria:Nations/'' followed by the nation's name, as in ''The World of Iceria:Nations/Alexandria''. It holds what the nation's articles leave out: who writes the nation and how to reach them, which stories other nations may join, what is settled and what is still a draft, and plans. A banner on the talk page of the nation's article links to it; the article itself does not. A meta page is never canon and is never cited as a reference. When something on it is settled, it moves into the articles.

An article describes only what has been settled. A plan, an idea under discussion or a page still to be written is left out until it exists, rather than described as if it already does.

{| class="wikitable"
! Out of the world !! Inside it
|-
| Mantua joined Iceria in October 2026. || ''Leave it out. The history section begins with Mantua's own past.''
|-
| Its creator placed the capital on the island's only crag. || Its capital, San Leopoldo en la Piedra, stands on the crag around the Basilica on the Rock.
|-
| The navy will be added later. || ''Leave it out until the navy has been written.''
|-
| The island is about the size of Malta. || ''Give its area in square kilometers instead.''
|}

=== 1.2 Neutral point of view ===
The encyclopedia takes no side, including the side of the nation it describes. It reports what a nation is, what it has done and what people in Iceria think of it; it does not admire or condemn. Where people disagree, the article says who holds each view and gives each view the weight it carries in the world.

* '''Attribute opinions.''' A judgment belongs to someone. "The reform was a disaster" is the writer's view; "The opposition in the Assembly called the reform a disaster" is a fact about the world.
* '''A nation's view of itself is a view.''' A national myth, an official history or a claim of descent is reported as what the nation believes or teaches, unless canon settles it.
* '''Write about neighbors as fairly as about your own nation.''' A war, a border dispute or a rivalry should be told so that the members on both sides would accept the account. Where another nation's own pages describe an event, follow them, unless a treaty or event agreed by every party settles it otherwise ([[Charter]] 1.4). Where the two nations' pages contradict each other, a steward settles it ([[Charter]] 3.8), and until then the article gives both accounts.

=== 1.3 Tense ===
Use the past tense for history and the present tense for what exists now: "Geneva is the capital", "the coup failed". A person who has died, a state that has fallen or a law that has been repealed takes the past tense throughout.

Anything that can change carries its year, so that the sentence stays true as time passes: "had about 81.7 million people in 2000", "Emperor since 1986". Words that are true only on the day they were written, such as ''currently'', ''now'', ''recently'' and ''today'', go out of date without anyone noticing. Replace them with a year.

=== 1.4 Plain, specific prose ===
Good encyclopedic writing is plain. It gives the reader facts in the fewest clear words and lets the facts make their own impression.

* '''Be specific.''' A number, a name or a date says more than an adjective. Not "a small island country with a pleasant climate" but "an island country of 1,200 km²".
* '''Prefer the plain verb.''' Write ''is'' rather than ''serves as'' or ''stands as'', and ''has'' rather than ''boasts'' or ''features''.
* '''Use the one exact word.''' A string of three adjectives usually means none of them is quite right. Instead of calling a people "proud, resilient and resourceful", say what they did.
* '''Name the doer.''' Use the active voice when it matters who acted: "the Assembly passed the law", not "the law was passed".
* '''Give each paragraph one topic,''' opening with a sentence that says what the paragraph is about. Avoid one-sentence paragraphs outside very short sections.
* '''State each fact once.''' Outside the lead, which summarizes the article (section 3), a fact belongs in one place, and other sections link or refer to it.
* '''Stop when the facts stop.''' A section ends with its last fact. It does not end with a sentence that sums up, looks ahead or tells the reader what it all means.

=== 1.5 Words to watch ===
None of the words below is forbidden, but each usually weakens an article. When you find one, check whether a plainer word or a fact would do the job better.

{| class="wikitable"
! Kind !! Watch for !! The problem !! Instead
|-
| Praise || ''legendary, renowned, iconic, world-class, breathtaking, vibrant, picturesque, prestigious, thriving, beloved,'' a ''rich'' history or heritage || It judges instead of describing. || Give the fact behind the praise: the age of the building, the number of visitors, the prize it won.
|-
| Inflated verbs || ''boasts, features, serves as, stands as, plays a key'' (or ''vital'', ''pivotal'') ''role, lies at the heart of, nestled'' || It dresses up a plain fact. || ''is, has, lies, contains''
|-
| Stock phrases || ''steeped in history, a land of contrasts, a melting pot, the crossroads of, a testament to, a tapestry of, the jewel of'' || It could describe any place at all. || Say what makes this place different from others.
|-
| Editorializing || ''notably, interestingly, importantly, remarkably, of course, clearly, undoubtedly, it should be noted that, it is worth mentioning'' || It tells the reader what to think. || Delete the word. If the fact matters, its place in the paragraph will show it.
|-
| Weasel words || ''some say, many believe, it is widely regarded as, critics argue, is considered'' || It hides who holds the view. || Name the holder: "the opposition in the Assembly", "the Imperial College of Arms".
|-
| Loaded verbs for "said" || ''claimed, admitted, insisted, revealed, conceded'' || It hints at doubt or guilt. || ''said, stated, wrote,'' or ''argued'' for an argument
|-
| Doubt || ''so-called, supposed,'' and ''alleged'' where no charge was made || It sneers. || Give the name plainly, and attribute any doubt (1.2).
|-
| Euphemism || ''passed away, gave his life, lost her battle with'' || It softens what happened. || ''died, was killed, died of''
|-
| Vague time || ''recently, currently, nowadays, today, in modern times, soon, in recent years'' || It goes out of date. || A year: "in 1998", "since 1994", "as of 2000"
|-
| Summing up || ''overall, in summary, in conclusion, ultimately,'' or "today it remains a symbol of…" || An encyclopedia reports; it does not conclude. || End with the last fact.
|-
| Dramatic contrast || ''not only… but also,'' "not just a city, but…", ''more than a mere'' || It is rhetoric, not report. || Say what the thing is.
|}

The same sentence, before and after:

{| class="wikitable"
! Before !! After
|-
| Nestled at the mouth of the Lère, the vibrant city of Port-Réal boasts a rich maritime heritage and stands as a testament to Alexandria's enduring spirit. Today, it remains a bustling hub of trade and culture. || '''Port-Réal''' is a port city in [[Alexandria]], at the mouth of the Lère, the river on which Geneva stands.
|}

The second version is shorter and tells the reader more: where the city is and what it is. Its trade, its history and its culture each belong in a section of their own, with figures.

=== 1.6 Wikipedia-grade ===
Every article on Iceria is written to the standard of a good Wikipedia article on the same kind of subject. Before drafting, look at how Wikipedia covers a subject of that kind, such as a French region for an Alexandrian region or a general election for an election, and give the article the sections and the depth such an article has, each section with prose of its own. Where this manual and Wikipedia differ, this manual wins (20), and an article still describes only what is settled (1.1): its sections are filled with settled facts, never with padding.

== 2. Article titles ==
=== 2.1 The common name ===
Title an article with the name a reader in Iceria would most likely look for. Use the singular ("Imperial Navy", not "Imperial Navies") and leave off a leading ''The'' unless it is part of the name every time it is used, as in ''The Compass''. Titles take sentence case, like headings: only the first word and proper names have capitals, so "List of nations in Iceria" but "Imperial Guard", which is a proper name.

The title should match the name in bold in the article's first sentence (section 3.1). Other names for the subject, such as an official name, a former name or a name in another language, can be made into redirects to the article.

=== 2.2 Nations ===
A nation's article takes the nation's short name, and its full official name goes in the first sentence and the infobox. The article is ''Alexandria'', not ''Empire of the Alexandrians''; ''Empire of the Alexandrians'' redirects to it.

=== 2.3 Telling same-named pages apart ===
Two nations may not share the name of a nation or of a capital, but they may share other names (World Standards 6.5). When two articles would have the same title, each adds the name of its nation in brackets: a town called Valmont in each nation would give ''Valmont (Alexandria)'' and ''Valmont (Mantua)''. Add the brackets only once a second article of that name exists or is being written; until then, the first article keeps the plain name.

=== 2.4 People and events ===
People's articles follow the naming rules in section 9.1, and events follow section 11.1.

== 3. The lead ==
=== 3.1 The first sentence ===
The first sentence names the subject in bold, exactly as the title gives it, and says what the subject is and where. A reader who stops after one sentence should know what the article is about. Other names in common use, such as the official name, come right after it, also in bold. The bold name carries no link.

Each type of article has its own pattern:

{| class="wikitable"
! Article !! Pattern !! Example
|-
| Nation || '''''Short name''''', officially the '''''official name''''', is a country in ''region'', on ''sea or ocean''. || '''Alexandria''', officially the '''Empire of the Alexandrians''', is a country in the south-west corner of Eostria, on the Thalassine Ocean.
|-
| Person || '''''Name''''' (born ''date'') is the ''role''. For the dead: (''date of birth'' – ''date of death'') and ''was''. || '''Edgard II''' (born 16 April 1960) is the Emperor of the Alexandrians.
|-
| Place || '''''Name''''' is a ''kind of place'' in ''nation'' (linked), ''location''. || '''Port-Réal''' is a port city in [[Alexandria]], at the mouth of the Lère.
|-
| Event || The '''''name''''' was a ''kind of event'' in ''place'', from ''date'' to ''date''. || ''See section 11.2.''
|}

Avoid openings that delay the subject, such as "This article is about…" or "Located in the south of…". The rest of the first paragraph adds the facts a reader needs next: for a nation, its size, population, capital and peoples; for a person, the dates and events that made them notable. For Ostrenna, a nation invented for this manual, the whole first paragraph might read:

<blockquote>'''Ostrenna''', officially the '''Kingdom of Ostrenna''', is an island country in the Thalassine Ocean, off the coast of Eostria. It covers 1,200 km², mostly low hills, and its capital is Vessa. It had about 240,000 people in 2000, most of them Ostrennans.</blockquote>

=== 3.2 What the lead covers ===
The lead is a summary of the article, and it can be read on its own. It covers the most important points of the body, in roughly the order the body gives them, with the most weight on what matters most. It is easiest to write last, once the body is finished.

Nothing appears only in the lead. Every fact in it is also in the body, where the full detail and its references live. A fact in the lead that the body states with a reference needs no reference of its own, though a figure or anything another member might question may carry one in both places.

=== 3.3 Length ===
The lead grows with the article: one paragraph for a short article, two or three for a full one, and no more than four for the longest. The lead has no heading. Only the short description, maintenance templates and the infobox come before it.

=== 3.4 The short description ===
Every article starts with a short description, a phrase that tells a reader what the article is about at a glance. It goes in the first line: <code><nowiki>{{SHORTDESC:Country on the continent of Eostria}}</nowiki></code>.

* Aim for about 40 characters; keep under 60.
* Start with a capital letter, and leave off a leading ''A'' or ''The'' and a final full stop.
* Avoid words that go out of date, such as ''current'' or ''former''. Give the years instead.

Examples from articles on Iceria:
* ''Alexandria'': Country on the continent of Eostria
* ''Mantua'': Island principality under Alexandrian protection
* ''Edgard II'': Emperor of the Alexandrians since 1986
* ''Edgard I'': Emperor of the Alexandrians from 1959 to 1986
* ''House of Montillet'': Reigning house of Mantua

== 4. Layout ==
=== 4.1 Sections and headings ===
An article is divided into sections, each with a heading, so that a reader can find a topic from the table of contents. Headings follow the same rules as titles (2.1):

* '''Sentence case:''' "Government and politics", not "Government And Politics".
* '''Start at level two''' (<code><nowiki>== Heading ==</nowiki></code>), and go down one level at a time: a <code><nowiki>=== subsection ===</nowiki></code> sits under a section, never directly under the lead.
* '''Keep it plain.''' No links, bold, italics or templates inside a heading, and no full stop at the end.
* '''Do not repeat the title.''' In ''Mantua'', the heading is "Economy", not "Economy of Mantua". A subsection does not repeat its section either: under "Geography", the subsection is "Climate", not "Climate of the geography".
* '''Keep headings unique''' within an article, so that a link to a section always finds the right one.

A heading may carry a date range when the section covers a period, as in Mantua's "The Cavaletta lords and dukes (1406–1632)".

=== 4.2 The closing sections ===
After the body come the closing sections, always in this order, each only when it has something in it:

# '''See also''': a short bulleted list of related articles that the body does not already link.
# '''Notes''': explanatory footnotes (13.4).
# '''References''': the list of references (13.3).

An article has no ''External links'' or ''Further reading'' section. Its sources are pages on this wiki, and an encyclopedia inside Iceria has nothing outside it to point to.

=== 4.3 Prose, lists and tables ===
An article is written in prose. A reader learns more from a paragraph that connects its facts, saying which caused which and which matters most, than from a list of the same facts.

* '''Lists''' suit items that really are a list: the members of a council, a nation's public holidays, the related articles under ''See also''. A list never stands in for a section of prose.
* '''Tables''' suit data that the reader compares across rows: the regions of a nation with their seats, areas and populations, or the results of an election. Give a table a header row, and say in the prose what it shows.
* '''No bolded lead-ins''' in an article's prose. A paragraph that needs a label at its start needs a heading instead.

=== 4.4 Main articles and summary sections ===
When a topic has its own article, the section on it in a broader article is a summary, a paragraph or two, and points to the full article with <code><nowiki>{{Main}}</nowiki></code> on the line under the heading, as in [[Mantua]]:

<pre>
=== The sovereign ===
{{Main|House of Montillet}}
</pre>

The summary repeats the main points of that article in the same spirit as a lead (3.2), and nothing in the summary contradicts it. When the two disagree, the main article is the one to fix first, and the summary follows it.

== 5. Dates, numbers and units ==
=== 5.1 The calendar ===
Two real months are one year in Iceria, and each year has the usual twelve months ([[Charter]] 1.6). The world's story begins in the year 2000, and history before it may go back as far as a nation needs.

No event in an article is dated later than the current date in Iceria. To show the current date, use <code><nowiki>{{Iceria date}}</nowiki></code>. Until Iceria opens ([[Charter]] 3.11), the calendar has not started, and <code><nowiki>{{Iceria date}}</nowiki></code> shows no date: history ends with 1999, and figures such as a population are given for 2000. A nation's future, such as a coming election or a planned railway, is described only as a plan that people in the world have made, and only once it has been made.

=== 5.2 Writing dates ===
Dates are written day, month and year, with no comma and no ''th'': "14 March 1998" (World Standards 5.1).

{| class="wikitable"
! Kind !! Write !! Not
|-
| Full date || 14 March 1998 || March 14, 1998; 14th March 1998; 14/03/1998
|-
| Month and year || March 1998 || March, 1998
|-
| Range of years || 1406–1632 || 1406-1632; 1406 to 1632 in a heading
|-
| Range within a month || 14–18 March 1998 || 14 to the 18 March 1998
|-
| Range across months || 14 March – 2 April 1998 || 14 March–2 April 1998
|-
| Century || the 19th century, or the nineteenth century; a 19th-century church || the 1800s century; the XIXth century
|-
| Decade || the 1990s || the 1990's; the '90s
|-
| Something that changes || as of 2000 || currently; at present
|}

The en dash (–) in a range has no spaces around it, unless one side of the range already contains a space, as in "14 March – 2 April 1998". Centuries may be written in numerals or in words ("the twentieth century"), the same way throughout an article.

=== 5.3 Numbers ===
* '''Words or numerals.''' Spell out whole numbers from zero to nine and use numerals from 10: "three regions", "13 regions". Numbers that are compared in one sentence keep the same form: "between 5 and 12 ships".
* '''Never start a sentence with a numeral.''' Spell the number out, or rewrite the sentence.
* '''Separators.''' Use a comma for thousands and a full stop for decimals: "1,200 km²", "1.50".
* '''Large numbers''' take a numeral and a word: "81.7 million people", "2.12 trillion IRU". Round to the precision the source gives, and add ''about'' when the figure is an estimate.
* '''Percentages''' use the sign with no space: "8%", "about 2.3% of GDP".

=== 5.4 Currencies ===
Each nation has its own currency, or shares one with other nations by agreement (World Standards 8.1). Name the currency in full at its first mention, as in "the Alexandrian écu", and give amounts after the number, in the currency's own name: "4 billion écu". After a plain number the name takes its plural, as in "400 écus"; after million or billion it stays singular, as in "3 billion écu".

To let readers compare economies, figures for a whole economy, such as gross domestic product, government budgets and military spending, are also given in International Reference Units (IRU), the shared unit for comparison. One IRU buys about what one US dollar bought in 2000. Give the IRU figure first, then the nation's own: "about 6 billion IRU, some 4 billion écu". Prices and wages inside a nation may be given in its own currency alone.

=== 5.5 Units ===
Use metric units: kilometers, square kilometers, meters, kilograms, degrees Celsius (World Standards 5.3). Distances at sea may be given in nautical miles, with kilometers after them the first time: "120 nautical miles (222 km)". Do not convert to other units.

Write a unit symbol after a numeral, with a space between them: "120 km", "1,200 km²", "18 °C". In prose without a number, spell the unit out: "several kilometers inland".

The area of a nation, or of a region within it, is the figure the Claims Desk records (World Standards 5.4). Use that figure rather than one you have measured yourself, so that every article gives the same number.

== 6. Spelling, capitals and punctuation ==
=== 6.1 Varieties of English ===
Iceria's writers come from many places, and any established variety of English is welcome: American, British, Canadian, Australian and others. The rules are about consistency:

* '''One variety per article.''' An article does not mix ''color'' and ''colour'', or ''center'' and ''centre''.
* '''An article keeps its variety.''' The variety its first major writer chose stays, and an edit that changes only spelling is reverted.
* '''Names keep their own spelling.''' An office, institution or work named by its creator keeps that spelling in every article, whatever variety the article around it uses: a Ministry of Defence stays a Ministry of Defence in an American-spelled article.
* '''Quotations keep their spelling''' (section 16).

=== 6.2 Capital letters ===
Capitals mark proper names. A word that is not part of a name stays in lowercase, even when it refers to something important.

{| class="wikitable"
! Kind !! Capital !! Lowercase
|-
| Titles and offices || Emperor Francis Joseph III; the title Emperor of the Alexandrians || the emperor; an emperor; three chief ministers
|-
| Institutions || the Imperial Navy; the National Council || the navy; the council
|-
| Peoples and languages || Alexandrians; Martino; Lerian ||
|-
| Directions and regions || the Crown Isles, a named group || the south-west corner of Eostria; the northern coast
|-
| Seasons, and words for kinds of things || || a summer drought; a monarchy; a republic
|}

A title is capitalized before a name and when it is given in full as the name of the office. Standing alone, it is lowercase, even when it refers to one person: "the emperor".

=== 6.3 Bold and italics ===
* '''Bold''' is used in an article only in the lead, for the title and its other names (3.1). Do not bold words for emphasis.
* '''Italics''' are for the titles of books, newspapers, films and other works; for the names of ships; for words that are not English, such as the motto ''Non sine sole iris''; and for a word or name mentioned as itself, as in "the name ''Alexandria''". Do not italicize proper names in other languages: San Leopoldo en la Piedra is a place name, not a foreign phrase.
* Do not use italics for emphasis either. A sentence that needs emphasis can usually be rewritten to put the important word last.

=== 6.4 Punctuation ===
* '''Quotation marks.''' Use straight double quotation marks, with single quotation marks for a quotation inside a quotation: "the motto 'no rainbow without the sun'". Apostrophes are straight too.
* '''Punctuation and quotes.''' A full stop or comma goes inside the quotation marks only when it belongs to the quoted words: the citizen-soldiers were called the ''alexandri'', "defenders of men".
* '''The serial comma''' (the comma before ''and'' in "tin, copper, and nickel") may be used or not, but the same way throughout an article.
* '''Dashes.''' In running prose, use commas, colons, parentheses or a new sentence rather than a dash. A pair of commas or parentheses sets off an aside; a colon introduces an explanation or a list. This is Iceria's own rule, and it keeps prose plain.
* '''The en dash''' (–) appears only in ranges: years, dates, numbers and page numbers (5.2). Between words joined to make one modifier, use a hyphen: "a 19th-century railway".
* '''Hyphens''' join compound modifiers before a noun ("a well-known poet") and not after it ("the poet is well known").

=== 6.5 Abbreviations ===
Spell out a term at its first use, with the abbreviation in parentheses after it, then use the abbreviation: "gross domestic product (GDP)", "International Reference Units (IRU)". Write abbreviations without full stops: IRU, not I.R.U. Common units (km, kg) and abbreviations that a reader would know at once need no spelling out.

In running prose, write ''for example'', ''that is'' and ''and so on'' rather than ''e.g.'', ''i.e.'' and ''etc.''

== 7. Names and languages ==
=== 7.1 Place names fit the nation ===
A nation's place names fit its own language, whether that language is invented or inspired by a real one (World Standards 6.1). In Alexandria, whose languages are Alexandrian and Martino, the names are Port-Réal, Villalba and Sainte-Aube; in Mantua, San Leopoldo en la Piedra. A name from another language is fine when the article explains it, as a name left by settlers, traders or a former ruler.

=== 7.2 Real names ===
No nation takes the name of a real country, so the map never reads as Earth with new labels (World Standards 6.2). Other real names, among them the names of real cities, may be used where they fit the nation's language: Alexandria's capital is Geneva.

=== 7.3 The fixed names, and naming a new feature ===
The names of the continents, the polar regions, the oceans and seas, and the largest lakes, islands, rivers and mountain ranges on the world map are fixed (World Standards 6.3). Use them exactly as the world map spells them, such as Eostria, the Thalassine Ocean, Hyperbora and The Austrine. A nation's own name for one of them may follow in parentheses, as a native name (7.4).

A feature the world map does not name, such as a smaller river, a bay or a mountain, is named by the first nation to publish a name for it on the wiki (World Standards 6.4). Before naming one, search the wiki and the [[Atlas of Iceria]] to see whether it already has a name. Once a feature has a name, every article uses it.

=== 7.4 Native names and words in other languages ===
Give a subject's name in its own language when it differs from the English one, in parentheses and italics, with the language named: "the Lère (''Lera'' in Martino)". Use the English name in the title and the text when one is in common use; use the native name when it is not.

A word or phrase in another language is in italics, with its translation in quotation marks at its first use: "the ''alexandri'', 'defenders of men'". After that it may stand alone. A word that has become ordinary English needs neither italics nor a translation.

== 8. Nations ==
A nation's article is where a reader new to the nation starts, and every other article about the nation links back to it. It follows one shape in every nation, so that a reader who knows where the economy is in one nation's article can find it in the next.

=== 8.1 The lead ===
The first sentence follows the pattern in 3.1. The rest of the lead summarizes the article in the order the body gives it, in up to four paragraphs:

# Where the country is, its area and its population with their year, its capital and largest city, and its peoples.
# Its history in a few sentences: how it formed and the turns that made it what it is.
# Its government: the form of state, the head of state, the head of government and the legislature.
# Its economy and its place among the nations: what it lives on, its currency, and its allies or protector.

The lead of [[Alexandria]] has these four paragraphs. A nation with a short article may fold them into one or two, as [[Mantua]] does.

=== 8.2 The infobox ===
Every nation's article carries <code><nowiki>{{Infobox Country}}</nowiki></code>, after the short description and before the lead. Its fields take the names Wikipedia uses for its own country infobox, and [[Template:Infobox Country]] lists and explains them all.

* '''Leave out a field that does not apply''' rather than writing "none", "N/A" or "unknown". Every field is optional.
* '''The area is the Claims Desk figure''' (5.5), in the box and in the text alike.
* '''The population and the GDP carry their year''', the same year the text gives them.
* '''The GDP is in International Reference Units''' (IRU).
* '''The time zone is counted from the prime meridian.'''
* '''The locator map''' goes in ''image_map'', drawn from the claims map (15.2).
* '''The tier and the status are set by the stewards.''' Leave them as the stewards set them.

The box repeats the article and never holds a fact the body lacks (14.2). It also files the page in [[:Category:Countries]].

=== 8.3 Section order ===
Use these sections, under these headings and in this order. Leave out a section the nation has nothing for yet, but do not rename or reorder the ones it has. The subsections are the writer's choice; the ones below are [[Alexandria]]'s.

{| class="wikitable"
! Section !! What it covers !! Subsections in Alexandria
|-
| Etymology || Where the nation's name comes from, and its other names. A short article may fold this into History. ||
|-
| History || From the first settlement to the current year, in subsections by period. || Leria; The kingdom and the crown at Geneva; The age of sail; Revolution and Imperial Restoration; The twentieth century
|-
| Geography || The land, its rivers, coasts and islands, its climate and resources, and the state of its environment. || Climate; Natural resources; Environment
|-
| Government and politics || The constitution, the head of state and of government, the legislature, the courts, the regions, foreign relations and the armed forces. || Legislature; Law and courts; Regions; Foreign relations; Military
|-
| Economy || What the nation produces and trades, its currency and its infrastructure. || Agriculture; Industry and mining; Transport; Energy; Tourism
|-
| Demographics || The population, its cities, languages, religions and schools. || Cities; Languages; Religion; Education
|-
| Culture || The arts, food, festivals, media and sport. || Cuisine; Festivals; Arts and architecture; Literature; Media; Sport
|}

The closing sections (4.2) follow. A subsection that carries unusual weight for one nation may become a section of its own, placed after the section it came from: Mantua, a protectorate, gives ''Foreign relations and defense'' its own section after ''Government and politics''.

=== 8.4 A finished nation article ===
A nation's article counts as finished when it has all of these:

* a short description (3.4) and an Infobox Country with a locator map;
* a lead that follows 8.1;
* at least the History, Geography, Government and politics, and Economy sections, each with prose of its own;
* a reference for its area, its population and every other figure, except a figure the article itself publishes first (13.2);
* its categories (17).

A [[Good articles|good article]] asks for more: the checklist in section 19.

== 9. Biographies ==
=== 9.1 Titles ===
A person's article takes the name they are most commonly known by. Commoners take their given name and surname, without titles or ranks: ''Matthieu Poiters'', not ''First Consul Matthieu Poiters''. Royalty and nobility follow Wikipedia's naming conventions:

{| class="wikitable"
! Person !! Pattern !! Example
|-
| Emperor or king || Name and regnal number || Edgard I; Edgard II
|-
| Sovereign prince or princess || Name and number, then the title || Amalio III, Prince of Mantua; Fabiola, Princess of Mantua
|-
| Member of a royal house with a peerage || ''Prince'' or ''Princess'', name, then the peerage || Prince Nicolás, 1st Baron de Ourenca; Princess Elida, Countess Agia
|-
| Holder of a peerage || Name, then the peerage || Carlos, Baron de San Pablo
|}

An emperor or king takes the name alone only while no other monarch on the wiki has the same name and number. Once two do, both titles add their realm (2.3).

=== 9.2 The lead ===
The lead gives the name in bold, the person's full name in italics in brackets when it differs, the dates of birth and death, and the role that makes them notable. The rest of the first paragraph tells the reader why they matter.

For Ostrenna, the invented nation of 3.1:

<blockquote>'''Sigrun II''' (''Sigrun Maria Teresa''; born 4 March 1951) is the Queen of Ostrenna.</blockquote>

A living person who has left an office stays in the present tense, and only the office is past: "'''Edgard I''' (born 24 November 1934) was the Emperor of the Alexandrians from 14 May 1959 until he abdicated on 15 June 1986." A dead person takes both dates, with an en dash between them, and the past tense throughout.

=== 9.3 Names in the text ===
After the first mention, a commoner is called by surname alone ("Poiters"), and a member of a royal house by given name ("Fabiola"), with the regnal number added where two people share a name ("Edgard I" and "Edgard II"). A noble may be called by title once the article has given it: "the baron".

Leave styles of address out of the prose. Words such as ''Her Serene Highness'' or ''His Imperial Majesty'' belong in a ''Titles and styles'' section, as in [[Fabiola, Princess of Mantua]], and nowhere else in the article. A title standing alone is lowercase (6.2): "the princess", "the emperor".

=== 9.4 The living, the dead, and other members' characters ===
A biography follows the tense rule in 1.3: the present for the living, the past for the dead and for everything they did.

Every member writes only their own nation and its people ([[Charter]] 3.8). An article does not give a person from another member's nation a crime, a scandal, an illness, a death or a private life that the member has not written or agreed to. In the same way, no article makes another member's nation a colonizer, an enslaver or a wartime enemy without that member's agreement ([[World Standards]] 13.6). A biography may report criticism of a foreign leader, attributed to the people who made it (1.2), but never as a way to attack the member who writes that leader ([[Charter]] 6.1: nations may insult each other; people may not).

=== 9.5 Infoboxes and succession boxes ===
* '''<code><nowiki>{{Infobox Person}}</nowiki></code>''' goes on every biography, after the short description. It holds the name, title and full name; the dates and places of birth and death; the royal house, parents, spouse and children; the office, its term, and the predecessor and successor; and the person's religion and occupation. Write a term as a range with an en dash (5.2): "5 May 1978 – present".
* '''<code><nowiki>{{Infobox royal house}}</nowiki></code>''' goes on the article about a dynasty, such as [[House of Montillet]], not on its members' biographies.
* '''Succession boxes''' (<code><nowiki>{{S-start}}</nowiki></code>, <code><nowiki>{{S-bef}}</nowiki></code>, <code><nowiki>{{S-ttl}}</nowiki></code>, <code><nowiki>{{S-aft}}</nowiki></code>, <code><nowiki>{{S-end}}</nowiki></code>) may close the article of an office-holder, after the references. They are optional, because the infobox already names who came before and after.

<pre>
{{S-start}}
{{S-bef|before=[[Amalio III, Prince of Mantua|Amalio III]]}}
{{S-ttl|title=Sovereign Princess of Mantua|years=1978–present}}
{{S-aft|after=Incumbent}}
{{S-end}}
</pre>

=== 9.6 Section order ===
A biography is told in the order of the life:

# '''Early life''': birth, family and education.
# '''Career''', or '''Reign''' for a sovereign: what made the person notable, in subsections by period when it is long.
# '''Personal life''', or '''Marriage and family''': marriage, children and private interests, as far as the member who writes the person has written them.
# '''Titles and styles''', and '''Honors''': for royalty and nobility, and for anyone decorated.
# '''Death and legacy''': for the dead.

Then the closing sections (4.2). A heading may name a stage of the person's own story instead, as [[Fabiola, Princess of Mantua|Fabiola]]'s article does with "Heir to the throne".

== 10. Cities and places ==
=== 10.1 The lead ===
The first sentence names the place in bold, says what kind of place it is, and links its nation; the rest of the first paragraph gives its region, its population with the year, and what it is known for. As a pattern, built from the [[Alexandria]] article:

<blockquote>'''Port-Réal''' is a port city in [[Alexandria]], at the mouth of the Lère in the region of Baudrix. It had about 4.6 million people in 2000, the second-largest city in the country after Geneva.</blockquote>

A building or other named place takes ''The'' in the sentence when its name is used with it, though not in the title (2.1): "The '''Cavaletta Palace''' (''Palacio de Cavaletta'') is the official residence of the sovereigns of Mantua."

=== 10.2 Infoboxes ===
A city, town or village carries <code><nowiki>{{Infobox Settlement}}</nowiki></code>. Its ''type'' field says what kind of place it is (city, town, village), and its ''country'' field links the nation. A building carries <code><nowiki>{{Infobox building}}</nowiki></code> instead.

=== 10.3 Section order ===
For a settlement: '''History''', '''Geography''', '''Government''', '''Economy''', '''Demographics''', '''Culture''' and '''Transport''', then the closing sections. For a building: '''History''', '''Architecture''' and '''Use''', as in [[Cavaletta Palace]]. As with nations (8.3), leave out a section with nothing in it, and keep the order of the rest.

== 11. Events and history ==
=== 11.1 Titles ===
An event takes its common name when it has one that people in Iceria use. When it does not, the title says what happened, where and when, with the year first:

* '''Elections and referendums:''' year, nation and kind of vote, as in "1997 Alexandrian general election" and "1978 Alexandrian constitutional referendum".
* '''Coups, disasters and other single events:''' year, place or nation, and what happened, as in "1991 Alexandrian coup attempt".
* '''Wars, revolutions and treaties''' take the name the world knows them by, as in the Compact of Los Santos of 1534.

=== 11.2 The lead ===
The lead says what happened, where, when and with what result. As a pattern, built from the [[Alexandria]] article:

<blockquote>The '''1991 Alexandrian coup attempt''' was an attempt by the Gendarmerie to seize power in [[Alexandria]] on 3 April 1991. Its troops took the Cortes, but after midnight Emperor [[Edgard II]] broadcast in uniform from the Palatine Palace in Geneva, and the Dragons of the Imperial Guard arrested the plotters.</blockquote>

=== 11.3 Infoboxes ===
A war, battle, rising or coup carries <code><nowiki>{{Infobox military conflict}}</nowiki></code>. An election carries <code><nowiki>{{Infobox election}}</nowiki></code> and gives its full results in a table (4.3). Iceria has no infobox yet for other events; such an article goes without one.

=== 11.4 Section order ===
* '''An event:''' '''Background''', '''Course of events''' (subsections by phase or date when it is long), '''Aftermath''' and '''Legacy'''.
* '''An election or referendum:''' '''Background''', '''Campaign''', '''Results''' and '''Aftermath'''.

Then the closing sections (4.2). An event that touches several nations is a story they agreed to tell ([[Charter]] 3.8); its article follows that agreed account first, then what each nation's own pages say ([[Charter]] 1.4).

== 12. Links ==
=== 12.1 What to link ===
Link a word when following it would help the reader understand the article: a nation, a person, a place, an institution, or a term the article does not explain. Link it at its first mention in the body. The lead counts on its own, so a page linked in the lead may be linked again where the body first mentions it. An infobox, a table or a caption may repeat a link the text already has, because readers often go straight to them.

=== 12.2 What not to link ===
* Words every reader knows, such as ''river'', ''king'' or ''capital'', and dates and years.
* The article's own subject, including the bold name in the first sentence (3.1).
* The same page twice in one section.
* Anything inside a heading (4.1) or a quotation (16).
* Two links side by side, which read as one: rewrite the sentence so that they are apart.
* Sites outside this wiki. An article links only to pages here (4.2).

=== 12.3 Pages not yet written ===
A link to a page nobody has written yet shows in red. A red link is fine while you draft, and it may stay in a finished article when the page is one that should be written. By the time an article is finished, prefer links to pages that exist. A missing page means only "not written yet" ([[Charter]] 1.4), never that the person, place or thing does not exist, so never infer a fact from a red link.

Before you write the page a red link points to, check that it is yours to write. A page about another member's nation belongs to that member ([[Charter]] 5.6).

=== 12.4 Piped links and links to sections ===
A piped link shows text that differs from the title it leads to: <code><nowiki>[[Fabiola, Princess of Mantua|Princess Fabiola]]</nowiki></code>. Pipe a link only when the sentence needs different words, and keep them close to the title, so that the reader can guess where the link goes. Where the title already reads well, link it plainly: <code><nowiki>[[Mantua]]</nowiki></code>, never <code><nowiki>[[Mantua|Mantua]]</nowiki></code>.

A link in an article's text leads to another article. It never leads to a proposal or a project page, even piped: write <code><nowiki>[[Mantua]]</nowiki></code>, not <code><nowiki>[[Proposal:Mantua|Mantua]]</nowiki></code>. Proposals are cited in the references instead (13.1).

A link may point to a section when the section covers the topic and no article does: <code><nowiki>[[Alexandria#Military|the Alexandrian military]]</nowiki></code>. Headings are unique within an article (4.1), so the link finds its section. When you rename a heading, check the pages that link to it. A link to a redirect works, so there is no need to change it into a piped link.

== 13. References ==
=== 13.1 What a reference may cite ===
Every reference points to a page on this wiki, because canon lives on the wiki ([[Charter]] 1.4). When sources disagree, the order of authority in [[Charter]] 1.4 decides.

The pages most often cited are the nation's proposal, its own articles, its stories in The Compass, the [[World Standards]], the official maps (World Standards 7.4), and the shared pages on the world, such as [[Geography of Iceria]] and [[History of Iceria]]. For a fact about a nation, its accepted proposal, its entry in the [[Local canon register]], and its own pages and Compass stories decide, below the higher sources in [[Charter]] 1.4, and no one writes facts for a nation that is not theirs ([[Charter]] 3.8).

Some places are never cited: a nation's meta page (1.1), talk pages, the [[Water cooler]], Discord, and any site outside this wiki. What happens on Discord counts only once it is written on the wiki ([[Charter]] 1.4); cite the page it was written on.

Cite the page that holds the fact, and the section of a long page: a Compass story rather than its month's page, and "[[World Standards]], section 8" rather than the World Standards alone.

=== 13.2 What needs a reference ===
* Every figure: an area, a population, a GDP, a budget, the strength of an armed force.
* Every date that another page could contradict, such as a founding, a reign or a treaty.
* Any fact that touches another nation, or that another member might question.
* Every quotation (16).

A figure that a nation publishes first in its own article needs no reference there: that article is its source, and every other page that gives the figure cites that article. If the nation's proposal already gives the figure, cite the proposal. The area of a nation, or of a region within it, is never published first in an article: it is the figure the Claims Desk records (5.5).

A fact in the lead that the body states with a reference needs none of its own (3.2). A paragraph drawn from one source carries the reference once, at the end of the sentence the source most supports, rather than after every sentence.

=== 13.3 How to write one ===
References are named and gathered under the References heading. Each source gets a short name, lowercase with hyphens for spaces. Its full reference is written once, inside the <code><nowiki><references></nowiki></code> tags at the end, and the text cites it by name wherever it is used. For the invented Ostrenna of 3.1, it looks like this:

<pre>
It had about 240,000 people in 2000.<ref name="proposal" />
…
== References ==
<references>
<ref name="proposal">[[Proposal:Ostrenna]], the nation's proposal.</ref>
<ref name="history">[[History of Ostrenna]], section 2.</ref>
</references>
</pre>

* '''Place the reference''' straight after the punctuation, with no space before it: ''in 2000.<nowiki><ref name="proposal" /></nowiki>''
* '''Write the full reference''' as a link to the page, then the section where it helps, ending with a full stop: <code><nowiki>[[World Standards]], section 8, "Economy yardsticks".</nowiki></code>
* '''Use every name you define.''' A name defined under References but never cited in the text, or cited but never defined, shows an error on the page.

=== 13.4 Notes ===
A note explains; a reference shows where a fact comes from. Put an aside that would interrupt the prose, such as another spelling, a conversion or how a figure was counted, in a note. Notes take their own group, so that they are numbered apart from the references:

<pre>
A sentence of the article.<ref group="note">An aside that would interrupt the sentence, such as another spelling of a name.</ref>
…
== Notes ==
<references group="note" />
</pre>

The Notes section comes before References (4.2). Keep notes few and short: a note that runs to a paragraph belongs in the text.

=== 14.2 The infobox repeats the article ===
The infobox is a summary of the article, like the lead. Every fact in it is also in the body, with its reference there if it needs one (13.2), so the box needs no references of its own. Give each figure exactly as the body does, with the same year. When the body changes, change the box with it.

=== 14.3 Fields that do not apply ===
Leave a field out when it does not apply or has not been settled. Do not write "none", "N/A", "unknown" or a dash, and do not fill a field with a guess. A field waits for its fact in the same way that an article waits for a plan to happen (1.1).

== 15. Images, maps and captions ==
=== 15.1 Uploading ===
Upload every image to this wiki through [[Special:Upload]]. Never use an image hosted elsewhere, because outside links break; claim images must always be uploaded here ([[Charter]] 3.6). Use only images you made yourself, or have permission or a compatible license to use, and credit whoever made them ([[Charter]] 5.5). Text and images here are published under the CC BY-SA 4.0 license unless a file says otherwise ([[Charter]] 5.1).

Name the file for what it shows, starting with the nation: ''Alexandria-regions.png''. Every file gets a description page with a summary of what the image shows, its source, and at least one category, as the maps of Alexandria do:

<pre>
== Summary ==
The regions of Alexandria, each with its seat.

== Source ==
Made from Iceria's canonical geography and Alexandria's national record. Used on [[Alexandria]].

[[Category:Maps of Iceria]]
</pre>

Flags are usually rectangles in a 2:3 or 1:2 ratio, uploaded as SVG or as a PNG at least 600 pixels wide (World Standards 7.3).

=== 15.2 Maps ===
A map of a nation's land is drawn from Iceria's two official maps: the world map for its coasts and named features, and the claims map for its borders. The official maps win wherever a nation's own map disagrees (World Standards 7.4). A nation may map its regions, its cities or its climate in as much detail as it likes, but its coasts, borders and named features follow the official maps. A map that changes the mapped geography needs a proposal, a stewards' decision and a new version of the world map first ([[Charter]] 3.6). Maps go in [[:Category:Maps of Iceria]], and a nation's locator map, drawn from the claims map, goes in its infobox (8.2).

=== 15.3 Captions ===
A caption says what the image shows, and when, if what it shows changes over time. Captions from [[Alexandria]]:
* ''Physical map of Alexandria''
* ''The regions of Alexandria''
* ''Population density, 2000''

A caption that is a fragment, like these, takes no full stop; a caption that contains a full sentence ends with one. Leave out openings such as "A picture of" or "Image showing", and keep praise out of captions as out of the text (1.5). A caption is part of the article, so it stays inside the world (1.1).

=== 15.4 Placement and size ===
Place an image at the top of the section it illustrates, as a thumbnail: <code><nowiki>[[File:Alexandria-regions.png|thumb|upright=1.4|The regions of Alexandria]]</nowiki></code>. Thumbnails sit on the right. Make an image larger with ''upright'' (Alexandria's maps use ''upright=1.4'') rather than a size in pixels, so that it scales on every screen. Do not stack images so tightly that they push into the next section: a short section takes one image at most.

== 16. Quotations ==
=== 16.1 Short and long quotations ===
A short quotation runs in the text, in quotation marks (6.4). A quotation of 40 words or more, or of more than one paragraph, is set apart as a block quotation, with no quotation marks:

<pre>
<blockquote>The words quoted, as they were written.</blockquote>
</pre>

A quotation keeps the wording and spelling of its source (6.1). Mark words left out with an ellipsis (…) and words added for clarity with square brackets.

=== 16.2 Attribution ===
Say in the text who said or wrote the words, and give the reference (13.2). A quotation belongs to a person or a text in Iceria: a speech, a law, a treaty, a story in The Compass. A quotation from the real world, or from a member, would step outside the world (1.1).

=== 16.3 Quotations and neutral prose ===
The article reports in its own voice, and a quotation illustrates what it reports. Do not use a quotation to praise the subject or to make an argument the article could not make itself (1.2), and do not build a section out of quotations. Leave out pull quotes and decorative quotation boxes.

== 17. Categories ==
Categories let a reader find every page on one subject: everything about Mantua, every treaty, every nation on one continent. They work best when pages are filed in similar ways, so this section offers a common pattern, modeled on Wikipedia's.

Every page has at least one category, placed at the very end of the page, after the references and any succession boxes.

=== 17.1 The two trees ===
[[:Category:Iceria]] holds the world: its nations, places, people and history. [[:Category:Meta Iceria]] holds the pages about the project, such as the [[Charter]], proposals, templates and this manual. An article never goes in Meta Iceria, and a project page never goes anywhere under Iceria.

Under Iceria sit:
* [[:Category:Countries]], which holds every nation article and every nation's own category.
* [[:Category:Continents]], which holds a category for each continent, and Polar regions, which holds the two polar regions. Every nation's category also sits in the category of each continent or polar region it lies on.
* The topic categories: "Geography", "History", "Politics and government", "Economy", "Military", "Culture", "People" and "Organizations". They hold the pages that belong to no single nation (17.5), and the topic categories of each nation that has split its own (17.3).
* [[:Category:The Compass]], for the newspaper.

=== 17.2 One category per nation ===
Every nation has one category, titled exactly as its article: [[:Category:Alexandria]], [[:Category:Mantua]]. A steward makes it once the nation's claim passes and the nation is provisional ([[Charter]] 3.2), writes its description, and files it in Countries and in each continent or polar region the nation lies on. If the nation is then declined, or its proposal returns to draft ([[Charter]] 3.4), the steward removes it. If your nation has no category yet, ask a steward rather than making one.

Every article about a nation's subject goes in that nation's category:

<pre>
[[Category:Mantua]]
</pre>

A small nation's pages can all sit in its one category. Once it grows, you can split it up as suits your nation; 17.3 has a common set to start from.

The nation article itself is filed in Countries by its infobox, so do not add that by hand. It is also filed in its own nation's category, with a space after the bar so that it sorts first:

<pre>
[[Category:Mantua| ]]
</pre>

=== 17.3 When a nation's category grows ===
Once a nation's category gets crowded (around 20 pages is a good point), its member or a steward can split it. These subcategories, each named for the nation, are a good starting set:

{| class="wikitable"
! Subcategory !! Holds
|-
| People of Mantua || biographies
|-
| Politics of Mantua || offices, parties, elections and laws
|-
| History of Mantua || events, wars and eras
|-
| Geography of Mantua || cities, regions and natural features
|-
| Military of Mantua || armed forces, units and bases
|-
| Economy of Mantua || companies, industries and the currency
|-
| Culture of Mantua || religion, languages, arts and sport
|-
| Organizations of Mantua || institutions that fit no row above
|}

Each one sits in the nation's category and in the matching topic category, so "Military of Mantua" is in [[:Category:Mantua]] and in Military, and "Politics of Mantua" is in Mantua and in Politics and government. Make only the subcategories the nation has pages for. Others, such as Regions of Alexandria or Elections in Alexandria, are fine when a nation has enough pages to fill them; file each one in the nation's category.

Using similar names across nations helps a reader who knows one nation's categories find their way around the next.

=== 17.4 The most specific category only ===
A page goes in the most specific category that fits it, and not also in the categories above that one. A page in "Military of Mantua" is not also in Mantua or in Military. The only exception is an article that has a category of its own, such as a nation or a continent, which is filed there with the space sort key as well (17.2).

A page filed twice shows up twice, and the broader category fills with pages that already have a better home.

=== 17.5 Pages that belong to no single nation ===
A continent, a sea, an international body or a treaty between nations goes in its topic category and never in one nation's category, even the nation that wrote it. A continent's article goes in its own continent's category (17.4); a treaty goes in [[:Category:Treaties]]; a body that several nations belong to goes in International organizations.

Filing such a page under one nation would hide it from readers of every other nation it concerns.

=== 17.6 Names and new categories ===
Category names use sentence case, like titles (2.1): "Cities and towns" and "Heads of state", not "Cities and Towns". A category of things takes the plural, as in "Treaties" and "Politicians". A nation's category takes the nation's name, and a topic takes its own name, as in "Geography" and "Military".

Use a category that exists before making a new one. A new category needs a line describing what belongs in it, and a broader category of its own. Any member may make a new category, including subcategories for their own nation. A nation's main category is made by a steward once the nation is provisional (17.2).

A reader who has learned one category name can then guess the next.

=== 17.7 Maintenance categories ===
Some categories track work rather than subjects, such as pages with a broken file link or templates' documentation. Templates and the wiki software add these, never writers. They are hidden from readers and sit under Meta Iceria, never under Iceria, so that a reader browsing the world sees only the world.

=== 17.8 Stewards tidy categories ===
Stewards may tidy the categories on any page to fit this section, and add one to any page that has none. They change only the category lines, never the article's text. If you disagree with a change, say so on the page's talk page.

To mention a category in the text without filing the page in it, put a colon before it: <code><nowiki>[[:Category:Countries]]</nowiki></code>.

== 18. Written works ==
Stories, poems, memoirs, letters, speeches and other writing set in Iceria have a home of their own, the Library. A page there is titled ''Library:'' and the work's title, as in ''Library:The Salt Road''. A work is not an article: it is something written inside the world, by a person who lives there, in their own voice. This section covers the page around the work. [[Help:Writing for the Library]] has a starter page to copy.

=== 18.1 What goes in the Library ===
Any writing a person in Iceria could have written belongs there: a short story, a novel, a poem, a play, a diary, a letter, a sermon, a speech or a memoir. Fiction and non-fiction use the same page.

A work follows the same rules as everything else on the wiki. Every member writes only their own nation ([[Charter]] 3.8), so a work is set in the writer's own nation, and it brings in another member's nation or people only with that member's agreement (9.4). Like an article, it does not contradict the official maps, the Charter, an agreed treaty or event or another nation's own pages (13.1).

=== 18.2 The page ===
A work's page has, in order:
# <code><nowiki>{{Infobox written work}}</nowiki></code>, which names the work's author inside the world. With no author given, the box reads Anonymous.
# One line in the encyclopedia's voice saying what the work is and who wrote it: ''The Salt Road is a short story by the Mantuan writer Livia Ferrante, written in 1641.''
# The text of the work.
# <code><nowiki>{{Written for Iceria|user=</nowiki></code>''your user name''<code><nowiki>}}</nowiki></code>, the credit to the member who wrote it.

The page is titled with the work's own title, capitalized as its author wrote it. The infobox files the page in [[:Category:Library]] and in its nation's category, or in that nation's culture category once the nation has split its own (17.3), so add no categories by hand.

The credit at the foot is the only place on the page that speaks about the project (1.1). In other articles, a work's title is written in italics (6.3) and links to its page: <code><nowiki>''[[Library:The Salt Road|The Salt Road]]''</nowiki></code>.

=== 18.3 The writer's voice ===
The rest of this manual governs only the frame around a work: its title, infobox, opening line and categories. The text itself follows the writer. It may use any voice and tense, any variety of English, dialogue, dashes, verse, invented spellings and headings of its own. The rules on dates (5.1) and on other members' nations (18.1) still apply, because they protect the world rather than the prose.

=== 18.4 Long works and series ===
A long work is split into chapters, each a subpage of the work named ''Chapter 1'', ''Chapter 2'' and so on: ''Library:The Salt Road/Chapter 1''. The work's own page keeps the infobox and the opening line. Put <code><nowiki>{{Chapter navigation}}</nowiki></code> at the top and foot of every chapter and at the foot of the work's page; it links each chapter to the one before and the one after, and files the chapters together. A chapter needs no infobox and no categories of its own.

Separate works that follow one another are a series. Give each its own page, and fill the infobox's ''series'', ''preceded_by'' and ''followed_by'' fields.

=== 18.5 Who edits a work ===
A work belongs to the member who wrote it. Anyone may fix a typo, a broken link or a category on it. Any other change to the text is suggested on the work's talk page, and the writer decides. Stewards tidy the frame as on any page (17.8).

=== 18.6 Articles about a work ===
A work needs no article of its own. When a work matters to its nation's story, such as a national epic or a famous memoir, an article about it may be written like any other article, with the work's page linked from it.

== 19. Before you call it finished ==
Go through this list before you call an article finished. It follows the four criteria for [[Good articles]], and each check names the section that explains it.

; Well written
* The first sentence names the subject in bold and says what and where it is, and the lead summarizes the body (3).
* The article stays inside the world: no members, proposals, claims, real-world dates or comparisons with places on Earth (1.1).
* It describes and attributes, and every word to watch has been checked (1.2, 1.5).
* It uses one variety of English, sentence-case headings, and no dashes in running prose (4.1, 6).
* Dates, numbers, currencies and units follow the house forms (5).
; Sourced
* Every figure, and every fact another member might question, has a reference to a page on this wiki, except a figure the article publishes first (13).
* Nothing contradicts the official maps, the Charter, an agreed treaty or event or another nation's own pages (13.1).
* Nothing is dated after the current date in Iceria (5.1).
; Broad
* It covers its subject as fully as a good Wikipedia article on the same kind of subject would (1.6).
* The article has the sections its type asks for, each with prose of its own (8.3, 9.6, 10.3, 11.4).
* It stays on its subject: detail that belongs to another article is a summary with a link (4.4).
; Illustrated
* Every image is uploaded here, with a description page and a category (15.1).
* Every caption says what its image shows (15.3).
* The infobox is filled in and agrees with the body (14).
; And last
* A short description (3.4), links to the pages that exist (12), and categories (17).

An article that passes every check is ready to be nominated as a [[Good articles|good article]].

== 20. Where this manual is silent ==
Where this manual says nothing, follow [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Manual_of_Style Wikipedia's Manual of Style], as far as it fits an encyclopedia written from inside the world (1.1). Where the two disagree, this manual wins. If the same question keeps coming up, raise it on this manual's talk page, so that the answer can be added here.
